The spectrum shows the amplitudes of different frequencies. This is not the same as the waveform plotted against position or time.

Try these steps

  1. Set the harmonic number to 1, then 2, then 5. Count cycles in the same horizontal interval. What happens to wavelength (or period)?
  2. Keep the harmonic number fixed. Change only amplitude. Does frequency change?
  3. Switch from sine to cosine. What changes about the waveform?
  4. Explain why the amplitude spectrum can consist of one bar even when the waveform has many peaks and troughs.
Check your reasoningHigher harmonic numbers have higher frequency and shorter wavelength/period; amplitude does not affect frequency. Cosine is shifted 90° relative to sine. One bar represents one frequency component, no matter how many cycles are displayed.

Try these steps

  2. Click Opposite. Why does the black resultant disappear?
  3. Try 90° apart. Is the amplitude of the result zero or twice the original?

Check your reasoningEqual-amplitude, equal-frequency waves in phase add to twice the amplitude; 180° apart they cancel exactly; 90° apart the resultant amplitude is √2 times either amplitude. Different frequencies cannot cancel everywhere at all positions.

T3-A1 · Wave amplitude and frequency
Changing amplitude changes wave height, not the number of cycles. A spectrum bar gives the strength of one harmonic/frequency component.

T3-A2 · Waves in and out of phase
Equal-amplitude waves with the same frequency add to twice the amplitude at 0° phase difference. At 180°, they cancel completely.

T3-A3 · Reading a spectrum
The discrete amplitude spectrum shows the relative strength of each harmonic/frequency component as a bar. The waveform plot instead shows signal amplitude as position or time changes.

Take-home messages

  • Amplitude changes a wave’s height; frequency changes how rapidly it oscillates. Relative phase determines whether equal-frequency waves reinforce or cancel.
  • An amplitude spectrum tells which frequencies are present and their strengths; the waveform describes how a signal varies with position or time.
